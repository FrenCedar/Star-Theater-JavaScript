Contacts: Facilities and Janitorial Services
If there is an issue with the physical environment surrounding the hardware and software in the star theater, facilities should be contacted. Normally, 
contact the star theater director, who will then contact facilities.  If the director can’t be reached right away, contact facilities at 606 783 2066.  After 
hours, you will be leaving a message with someone at the campus security desk who will attempt to leave a message with them. Contact should be 
initiated as follows:
1) If the temperature falls below 65 degrees or above 80 degrees.
2) If there is a leak of any kind…. Floor,  ceiling, or wall.  
3) If the full dome shows additional ‘staining’ (beyond what we already have).
4) If humidity is unusually high. 
5) If a visitor leaves a mark (vomiting, spilling, staining, leaving food, tracking in dirt), the janitorial services should be contacted.  We are supposed 
to have a janitor in the building during the day.   If you can’t find the janitor right away, look for the star theater director or Cindy to contact the 
Cleaning Supervisor Tim Ruley (606 316 0188).  If the neither the director nor Cindy can be found, you can contact the cleaning supervisor 
politely, particularly if it is a health hazard for folks in the building.

Full Dome Service Provider Evans and Sutherland: Contacting and Problem Solving
• In the event of a freeze (or other issues), first try the Reset button located in the top left of the leftmost monitor. If this does 
not resolve the issue, contact our service provider as described below.
• Evans and Sutherland is the service provider which develops, updates, and (upon request) maintains the Digistar system.
• Apply for an account here when you have your first issue. Click Support and fill out the application. Use your MSU ID and 
password for the username and password respectively. (You can also use a personal email if you choose, but using your 
MSU stuff proves easier.)
• Typically, several exchanges involving elaborating a problem, prescribing a potential solution that we can try on our end and 
describing the result will solve the problem. If not, we can set up an online session directly with E&S at the other end 
through 
• T eamViewer. Exchange mobile numbers with the E&S technician in advance.
• Use T eamViewer from the dropdown menu, get the user and password, update the ticket, and have a session for further 
issue resolution. TeamViewer will allow the technician to remotely control the system. They will be on call with you while 
troubleshooting. 
• In the event of an emergency (pertaining to the Digistar system) call (800)-327-5707 or (801)-588-7000. An emergency 
means that we are under time constraints, and a major issue is not able to be resolved.

Once they are on, you should see 3 different computers

one to the right is on its own system. The place where we 
will start is the 3 monitors on the left which give us access

should be on the home screen by default assuming the

Then, turn on the smaller monitor using the previously 
mentioned power button. Clove and Spice Automation will 
be open already.

Turning on Projectors​
Along the top of the lefthand monitor, switch to the SYSTEM tab 
(furthest right of the tabs as seen below surrounded by red box).
Now, turn on the projectors (indicated in red). Click on Power 
On.  It will prompt you to confirm turning on and shutting off the 
projectors, just select yes to move through the prompts. The light 
on the projector down I front will turn from red, to green, then 
white. White means on, red means off, green means in process. 
Once the projectors are on, a countdown will show on the dome. 
This is the auto warmup process. The projectors will take at 
least 20-30 minutes to warm up.
Note: As mentioned, projectors should be given 30 minutes to 
warm up.
Note:  If an error occurs (indicated by a red flashing exclamation 
point at the top right of the main Digistar 7 page) clear the error 
by right clicking it. (This error is common, its because the system 
for the projector loads slowly when you turn on Digistar.)

Turning off the System and the Projector
Make sure the projectors are turned OFF before you leave. Always check.  It only 
takes a second. Look at the projector and make sure the red light is on and the 
projector is clearly not projecting.
NOTE: Normally, the system will be on when you come in unless the system has 
been shutdown to protect it from severe weather or for maintenance. Assuming 
the crew is communicating, you should always know when the computer will be 
off and this will never line up with a show happening. As a side note, when 
leaving the Star Theater, just shut off the monitors (the same buttons on the back 
of the monitors you turned on when you came in), NEVER SHUT DOWN THE 
COMPUTER unless you know what you are doing. Consult a crew member 
veteran if you think this needs to be performed!

Laser Kosmos
This is an additional piece of controlling laser audio.
• Main function is to add base.
• Global bypass will ‘bypass’ or shutoff the base. This is useful if you want to use the shure mics.
• Base consists of ‘Quake’ and ‘Thud.’ Each of these should be adjusted for laser shows and turned off when using a shure mic. 
Additionally, you can simply bypass the base if you want to use a shure mic.

Auditorium/External Presenter Support
Aux Projector
• The aux projector is used for external presentations, this projector 
is separate from the ones we use for planetarium features. This is 
a single projector in the back that projects from 3 main inputs; the 
podium computer, laptop input, and doc cam. All of these and 
their input locations can be found at the podium.
• T o turn on the aux projector, use the right most monitor in the 
Spice Program; On and Off buttons. This is indicated in the red 
box to the right. T o make the display (Brightness 0) turn off 
without turning off the projector, use the respective buttons 
instead of the power buttons. If you need to fade for smooth 
transitions, although a bit hard, you can use the projector control 
sliders to do this manually.
• Alternatively, you can use the remote. Press the power button 
once to turn it on and twice to turn it off. 
• Optimal values for the projector are Brightness 50 and Contrast 
55. Y ou can set these values using the buttons.
11/28/2025 Star Theater Operators Manual 32

not working it is likely that a part of the aux projector has gone bad. In such a case report it to the current Director of the Star 
Theater immediately for repairs/replacement.

Advanced Scripting: Creating Custom Planets
It is possible to create your OWN celestial bodies, such as planets, stars, and other objects, in Digistar. Doing so can be unintuitive, so included 
here will be some explanations and examples showing how to create your own planet orbiting the Sun, your own star, and your own star system.
When creating a planet or body orbiting the Sun (in our own solar system), best practice will be to use a PlanetClass object. Further explanation of 
this class can be found in the Digistar user's manual (accessible via instructions located at the start of the scripting section). However, below is a 
brief code snippet showing what creating a new planet might look like, as well as some notes on what some of the less intuitive commands may be. 
The source for this example can be found at the directory locationC:\D7Content\Library\Scripts\Tutorials, alongside extraneous code that adds 
detail, but is not necessary for creating a custom planet.
# Setup Custom Planet (This section creates the class and sets the texture and radius of the planet)
 myPlanet is planetClass
 myPlanet texture $Content\Library\Images\Textures\planet_Dagobah.png
 myPlanet radius 0.8 rearth
# setup the scene graph (This section places the planet in the simulation. The order of these commands is CRUCIAL and must be followed in order to properly 
set the position of the planet.)
  ppos is emptyClass (An emptyClass is essentially a position marker for anything you wish to add to the scene or simulation)
  Sun add ppos (This sets the empty class as a "child object" of the Sun, meaning any subsequent position or attitude commands will be relative to the Sun!)
 ppos position 2.5 au (This string of commands will set the position of ppos ("planetposition") at a distance of 2.5 au (Earth distances from the Sun)
 scene add ppos (This command adds the position to the simulation)
 scene add myPlanet (This command will add the custom planet to the simulation)
 myPlanet goto ppos (This moves the planet TO THE POSITION YOU SET. If this command is NOT present, the custom planetwill reside INSIDE the Sun)

Advanced Scripting: Creating Custom Stars
When creating a custom star, it is best to use the sunClass. This allows several attributes to be applied that are not necessarily available to other 
classes of objects, such as glow, sunspots, and magnetic field lines. The following example creates and places a pulsar, but using different color 
and texture files or values, a custom star of any shape, mass, color, or design can be created.
#Initialize Position of PSR J0437
Pulspos is emptyClass
Pulspos position celestial 4.6167 -47.2524 509.8 ly (Using celestial coordinates is a great way to place an object "correctly" in the simulation universe. The 
order of numbers is right ascension, declination, and distance)
scene add Pulspos
PSRJ0437 is sunClass 
PSRJ0437 surfaceTexture $Content\Library\Images\Textures\pulsar-new.jpg (Again, this texture can be anything in the system or downloaded by the user.)
PSRJ0437 granulesFadeRange 1 1 (This command removes the convection cells on the star's surface. The first number sets the distance at which the granules 
are fully loaded, while the second sets the distance at which they BEGIN to load. Setting it as 1 1 deletes them, but this setting can be tweaked to the user's 
preference.)
PSRJ0437 color 100 100 100 (This essentially turns the model white. These are not typical RGB values as Digistar limits the value to 100, meaning user 
tweaking is necessary to achieve desired color.)
scene add PSRJ0437
PSRJ0437 goto Pulspos
Again, any star of any design can be created using the above code as a template.

Advanced Scripting: Creating an Orbiting Satellite
Digistar comes with the useful ability to simulate highly realistic satellite orbital motion, such as the ISS or Hubble. Doing so can be incredibly confusing as many 
commands are changed by use of the orbit and orbiter classes. Pay close attention to the commands and their order below, as misplacing even one of them can 
completely disassemble your orbiter and its orbital path.
#Establish classes for necessary objects
MagellanBody is solidModelClass (Required to make a physical object for the satellite)
MagellanBody model $Content\Library\Models\SolarSystem\Planets\Venus\Magellan Orbiter\Magellan.obj (Establishes the model used for the object)
MagellanBodyOrbit is orbitClass (This command creates the ORBITAL PATH AND TRAIL OF THE SATELLITE.)
MagellanBodyctl is orbiterClass (This command creates the ORBITER. Think of it like an emptyclass object. It does NOT have any appearance, but simply 
creates a point that follows the specified orbital path)
MagellanDot is dotClass (Not 100% necessary, but useful for spotting the satellite while at a large distance away. Creates a dot marker.)
#Assign the necessary body and orbit for the orbiter class
MagellanBodyctl body MagellanBody (This command sets the "physical appearance" of the orbiter as the solid model we established earlier. This can be ANY 
physical model, including planetclass or sunclass objects, which can be used to make orbiting planetary systems.)
MagellanBodyctl orbit MagellanBodyOrbit (This command sets the orbital path created earlier. This is a CRITICAL command. Not using it will not allow the 
code to continue as the orbiter won't recognize an orbital path for it to follow .)
#Set parameters of orbit and aesthetic characterisitics
MagellanBodyctl eccentricity 0.39177 (Establishes the apoapsis of the orbit. There is no command for this in Digistar. You MUST calculate the eccentricity to 
give the orbit an apoapsis.)
MagellanBodyctl periapsisDistance 6345.8 km (Sets the periapsis distance from the CENTER OF THE TARGET BODY)
MagellanBodyctl inclination 85.5 (Sets inclination from the ascending node)
MagellanBodyctl meanMotion 2658.4 (Can be calculated to give the satellite an accurate orbital speed. Measured in degrees/day)
MagellanBodyOrbit color beige (Not strictly necessary. Aesthetic only)
MagellanBodyOrbit perifocus Venus (Sets the target body for the orbit. Crucial for positioning anything you don't want to orbit the Sun)

• The audio is noticed to be fluctuating

(different websites, different browsers, digistar, base video player, etc.)
• Based on which conditions the issue is present under, this will 
help narrow down where the problem is located.  Is it present in 
all? Some? Only one?
• If it is in all of them, it may suggest something to do with the system as 
a whole or even a hardware issue
• If it is only in one, that suggests the issue is with that specific software

Backups
 What is a backup? Essentially, we copy the entire data of all 3 computers, Host, GP1, and GP2, onto either the “A” or “B” 
drives respectively. We swap which ones are actively used each backup to ensure even use of the SSD (Host) and disk drives 
(GPs).
   Why do a backup? A backup ensures that, even if the entire software of Digistar was deleted, we would be able to restore it. 
We also have smaller pseudo backups of individual shows which we hold onto incase of a show specific issue. For instance, 
the video file for a show may corrupt, and so we can simply slot it out and replace it with the copy.
   Additionally, we swap the drives that are used in order to ensure even use of both. This extends their lifetime use 
exponentially.
•  A backup should be performed about once per month. As you may have picked up on, it is very important that these are
done in case of large or even minor issues. There are three steps in a backup; Preparing the Hardware, Preparing the 
Software, and Post Backup Procedure.

Special Events: Valentines under the Stars
This romantic event for couples is held annually to celebrate Valentines Day, and involves 
• a four course gourmet meal with wine pairing and at (catered by Aramark)
• ‘at table service’ (no buffet) in the beautifully decorated rotunda,
• live music from the Lexington Symphony Harpist, 
• a full dome movie feature and live presentation of the night sky in the star theater. 
• Dessert is served after the show in the rotunda
• outdoor viewing of the night sky caps the evening as weather permits. 
• We also have a small romantic gift for the couple.  Such gifts have included 
• star maps of the sky on their most romantic evening 
• Beautifully illustrated books on constellations or other ‘sky’ features, 
• his and hers matching cosmic theme socks.  
T o our knowledge, this event is unique: no other planetarium in the world offers couples such an evening complete with a romantic meal. Space for 
seating is limited, so the number of couples is limited to 30. We have charged between $150 and $175 per couple, which includes everything listed 
above and free parking. 
The event is planned and organized with available crew members during the fall prior to the Holiday season, so that couples have the opportunity to 
purchase tickets as gifts. Steps involve:
• First of all, contacting Aramark in October to determine the exact date, discuss the gourmet menu, wine pairing, and determine cost per couple, 
plenty of time in advance. Based on the director’s experience and feedback from participants, the entrée should be braised or roasted with plenty 
of liquid to stay ‘moist’.  For example, the braised short ribs worked well. No greens so no conventional salad…greens just don’t stay fresh 
enough.  Use roasted vegetables, crostini, soup, instead.
• Contacting the harpist (Harping Sally) to make sure she is available for that date.
• Creating a flyer (see examples under the folder publicity) to be posted on our website (with a web update request), social media, emailed and 
posted.
• By early November, once a price per couple is obtained from Aramark to complete the flyer, contacting MSU billing and purchasing folks to create 
a website for registration and payment.

James Hood Organization (i.e., Mesmerica, Beautifica, Astronomica...) Shows
• Intermittently, we have had requests from the James Hood organization to do these shows. They contact the director and a contractual vehicle is 
created with MSU financial folks in the loop.  
• These operate very similarly to a normal show, except that instead of our normal setup, we use the "pre-show" show, before giving the following 
announcement, fade stop reset, and playing the show. Keep in mind that we need to setup 2 sets of stanchions, one to regulate flow into the 
hallway for dividing VIPs, and one to block off the stairwell. James Hood staff will handle all ticketing.
• Show Opening Script: “Hello and welcome to the Star Theater for this evening's special showing of MESMERICA.MESMERICAis a visual music 
journey created by the musician James Hood with animation by artists from around the world! This show is 50 minutes long; if at any point you 
feel overwhelmed by the visuals, please close your eyes and enjoy the music. In case you have to leave the theater, unfortunately we are unable 
to let you back in due to safety reasons, and we remind you not to leave any trash or belongings behind. Please no photos or recordings of any 
kind, especially flash photography, as the dome is very reflective. Additionally, please refrain from talking during the show as to not disturb the 
experience for the people around you, and make sure to silence your devices. Make a note that your chairs do recline and your emergency exits 
are the doors to your left and right. Without further ado, thank you for coming and, please, enjoy the show."
• Show Closing Script: "Everyone, I hope you enjoyed the show. If you have any questions about our other shows, such as Capcom Go or Edge of 
Darkness, please stop by and ask questions on your ways out! Thank you, and have a safe trip home."
• Notes: 
• These are not the attendees we normally work with. If an audience member is being especially loud, they are given one chance to be quieter after 
a warning from one of the James Hood staff. Alert them of this if you see it so they can handle it.
• If an audience member asks you for a special request, do not indulge, we have to keep in line with the program.
• If an audience member is being rude and aggressive towards you, stay calm, and alert staff.
• If you notice someone has food or drink, kindly remind them that these are NOT allowed in the theater.
• Due to the high quantity of people, it's more likely for us to encounter a wheelchair user. Ensure they enter the room first and use one of our 
wheelchair accommodating spots. Make sure they are comfortable and ready before anyone else enters.

External Communication
Website, Email Distribution, Marketing
Star Theater is a full-service state of the art digital planetarium operated by undergraduate students and directed by an MSU Space Science Center 
faculty and staff member for the purposes of education, entertainment, and social gatherings for campus and the surrounding communities. The Star 
Theater, a 100-seat digital theater, projects full 180º x 360º real-time blended video and graphics on a 40-foot dome screen with state-of-the-art surround 
sound.  In digital full dome theater mode, the all-dome video allows a broad range of programming, including simulated travel through deep space and 
the ocean's deepest depths, full motion and free movement through a 3D textured universe viewing endless astronomical phenomena, such as comets, 
planets, and supernovas, and 3D animations of biological, chemical, and physical systems.  PUBLIC DOMAIN External Videos and Images can also be 
projected onto the full dome. Our 2024 refurbished laser system also projects 3D images on the full dome. Our audio system has also been refurbished 
from 2024 to 2025 to support state of the art multi-media shows by the James Hood group.

as well as the regular public show schedule using their webupdate process. THEY decide whether it can be posted or not.  The director is working on 
getting the Morehead Tourism folks (or anyone in our email distribution) to create a ‘shadow’ website where we could post more material and particularly 
a more comprehensive events schedule.
